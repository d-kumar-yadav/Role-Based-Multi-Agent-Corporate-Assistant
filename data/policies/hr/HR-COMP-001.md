## Purpose
This document defines how compensation bands are structured and how the performance rating scale connects to compensation and promotion decisions, complementing the Performance Management section of the HR Employee Handbook.

## Compensation Band Structure
Each level (as defined in the Organization Hierarchy & Reporting Structure document) has a corresponding compensation band with a minimum, midpoint, and maximum, reviewed annually against market benchmarking data.

### Typical Positioning
| Level | Band Width | New‑Hire Positioning |
|-------|------------|----------------------|
| L1 — Associate | Min to Midpoint | 30th‑50th percentile of band |
| L2 — Mid‑level | Min to Midpoint | – |
| L3 — Senior | Min to Midpoint + 20% | 40th‑60th percentile of band |
| L4 — Lead/Manager | Min to Midpoint + 20% | 40th‑60th percentile of band (wide band, individually) |
| L5 — Principal/Director | Case‑by‑case benchmarked | – |
| L6 — Executive | Individually negotiated (case‑by‑case) | – |

Specific numeric band values are maintained in the Compensation Portal (restricted to HR Compensation team and Department Heads) and are not published in this document.

## Performance Rating Scale
| Rating | Label               | Typical Distribution |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| 5      | Exceptional         | ~5 % of employees |
| 4      | Exceeds Expectations| ~20 % of employees |
| 3      | Meets Expectations  | ~55 % of employees |
| 2      | Below Expectations  | ~15 % of employees |
| 1      | Unsatisfactory      | ~5 % of employees |

Ratings are calibrated across teams within a function to reduce manager‑to‑manager rating inconsistency, facilitated by the HR Business Partner before ratings are finalized and communicated.

## How Ratings Connect to Compensation
| Rating | Typical Merit Increase | Promotion Eligibility | Guidance |
|--------|------------------------|-----------------------|----------|
| 5 | Top of increase range + | Eligible | Bonus multiplier; eligible with 2nd consecutive rating |
| 4 | Above‑average increase | Not typically promotion‑eligible | – |
| 3 | Standard increase | Eligible this cycle | – |
| 2 | Minimal or no increase | – | Improvement Plan considered |
| 1 | No increase | – | Performance Improvement Plan required |

Actual increase percentages vary by year based on the Company’s overall compensation budget, approved annually by the CFO and Head of HR, and communicated to managers ahead of each review cycle.

## Promotion Process
Promotion to L4 (Lead/Manager) and above requires:
1. Two consecutive cycles rated 4 or above.  
2. A promotion packet prepared by the manager, reviewed by the Department Head.  
3. Calibration review with HR to confirm scope/impact matches the target level.  
4. Final approval from the Department Head and Head of HR.  

Promotions are typically processed twice yearly, aligned with the bi‑annual performance review cycle described in the HR Employee Handbook.

## Compensation Review Cycle
| Activity                         | Timing                              |
|----------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Performance reviews completed   | End of March / End of September |
| Calibration                      | First 2 weeks of April / October |
| Compensation decisions finalized | Mid‑April / Mid‑October |
| Communicated to employees        | Late April / Late October |
| Effective date of increase       | May 1 / November 1 |

## Off‑Cycle Compensation Adjustments
Off‑cycle adjustments (outside the standard review cycle) are made only in specific circumstances: a documented retention risk, a market‑correction identified through benchmarking, or a significant scope change (e.g., taking on a much larger team). All off‑cycle requests require Department Head and Head of HR approval, with Finance notified for budget tracking.
