## Purpose
This document lays out the exact structure (“topology”) of how a person is recruited at Meridian Global Technologies — who is involved at each stage, in what order, with what handoffs, and what decision authority each person has. It exists because the Recruitment & Hiring Policy explains the rules, but hiring managers and new recruiters repeatedly ask “who does what, in what order” — this document answers that directly.

## The Hiring Funnel, Stage by Stage
The recruitment process is a linear funnel with six stages. Each stage has one clear owner, even when multiple people are involved.

### Stage 1 — Requisition Creation  
**Owner:** Hiring Manager.  
The Hiring Manager raises a Position Requisition Form in the ATS, defining role, level, budget reference, and required skills. This is the entry point of the entire funnel — nothing downstream can start without an approved requisition.

### Stage 2 — Requisition Approval  
**Owner:** Department Head, plus Finance and HR Head for requisitions above the approved annual headcount plan.  
Approval typically takes 2‑3 working days. Once approved, the requisition is handed to the assigned Recruiter.

### Stage 3 — Sourcing and Screening  
**Owner:** Recruiter.  
The Recruiter sources candidates (careers page, LinkedIn, agencies, referrals, campus drives — see HR-RECRUIT-001, Section 4 for channel rules) and performs an initial resume screen, followed by a 30‑minute recruiter phone screen. Candidates who pass are handed to the Hiring Manager for the interview stage.

### Stage 4 — Interview Rounds  
**Owner:** Hiring Manager, supported by Interview Panelists.  
This stage itself has an internal structure — see Section 3 below for the panel topology by role level.

### Stage 5 — Decision and Offer Approval  
**Owner:** Hiring Manager proposes; HR approves and issues.  
The Hiring Manager submits a hire recommendation with panel scorecards attached. HR validates the recommendation against the panel’s aggregate feedback (minimum 75 % “hire” or “strong hire” required, per policy), checks the offer is within the approved compensation band, and generates the formal offer letter. Hiring Managers are not authorized to verbally commit to compensation at any point in this stage.

### Stage 6 — Background Verification and Onboarding Handoff  
**Owner:** HR, executed via third‑party BGV vendor.  
Once the candidate accepts, background verification runs in parallel with the candidate’s notice period at their current employer. On successful BGV completion, HR hands the candidate to the Onboarding workflow (see Section 5).

## Interview Panel Topology by Role Level
The number and type of interview rounds scales with seniority. This is the exact structure used for every requisition.

| Level | Hiring Manager Rounds | Recruiter Rounds | Technical Rounds | Culture/Values Rounds | Total Rounds | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Entry‑level (Associate) | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 3 | Combined with HM round |
| Mid‑level (2‑5 yrs) | 1 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 4 | — |
| Senior (5‑8 yrs) | 1 | 1‑2 | 1 | 1 | 5‑6 | May include cross‑lead or system functional design/strategy panelist |
| Lead | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 5‑6 | Requires at least one panelist from a different function |
| Director and above (including CXO) | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 5‑6 | Includes skip‑level and CXO head involvement |

*Panel composition rule:* every panel for Lead level and above must include at least one panelist from a function different from the hiring department, specifically to reduce evaluation homogeneity (also stated in the Recruitment & Hiring Policy, Section 11).

## Roles Involved and Their Exact Responsibility
| Role | Responsibility | Decision Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Hiring Manager | Raises requisition, runs hiring‑manager interview round, makes final hire recommendation | Cannot commit compensation |
| Recruiter | Sources, screens, schedules, screens out unqualified candidates, coordinates candidate communication | — |
| Interview Panelist | Conducts assigned interview round, submits structured scorecard within 24 hrs, provides independent hire/no‑hire score (does not decide alone) | — |
| Department Head | Approves requisition, budget and compensation; approves offers above band; can block offers that violate policy | Exception approval |
| HR Business Partner | Ensures compliance, issues offer letter | — |
| HR Head | Approves requisitions above headcount plan, final approval on exceptions, approves offers above band | — |

## Onboarding Handoff (Where Recruitment Ends and HR Operations Begins)
The moment BGV clears, ownership transfers fully from the Talent Acquisition team to the broader HR Operations team, who execute:

1. IT asset request triggered automatically (laptop, accounts — see IT Runbooks, Laptop Provisioning SOP).  
2. Facilities/workspace allocation.  
3. Buddy assignment by the Hiring Manager.  
4. Day‑1 orientation scheduled by HR Operations.  
5. Statutory documentation collected via the HR portal.  

This handoff is a hard boundary — Talent Acquisition’s involvement in that specific hire formally ends once the candidate’s Day‑1 orientation is scheduled.

## Typical End-to-End Timeline
| Stage | Typical Duration |
|---|---|
| Requisition creation to approval | 2‑3 working days |
| Sourcing to first interview | 5‑10 working days |
| Interview rounds (all stages) | 10‑15 working days |
| Offer decision to acceptance | 3‑7 working days |
| Background verification | 7‑14 working days |
| **Total (requisition to Day 1)** | Typically 5‑8 weeks (faster for entry‑level, slower for Director +) |

## Common Questions This Document Answers
- “What is the structure/flow to recruit a person?” — see Section 2, the six‑stage funnel.  
- “Who decides if someone gets hired?” — No single person; it’s the Hiring Manager’s recommendation validated against panel scorecards and approved by HR (Section 4).  
- “How many interview rounds does a candidate go through?” — Depends on level, see the table in Section 3.  
- “How long does hiring take end to end?” — See Section 6.  
- “Who is responsible after an offer is accepted?” — Ownership shifts to HR Operations for onboarding (Section 5).
